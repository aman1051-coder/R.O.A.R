About ROAR

Run by the young generation, for the young generation.

ROAR, the Rising Oncologists' Alliance in Radiation Oncology, is a national, non-profit, non-political professional body. It brings together radiation oncology residents and young radiation oncologists across India.

Vision · Article 6

To be the voice of the young generation of radiation oncologists in India. One united platform that combines academics with the real concerns of residents, so every resident gets the same quality of training, exposure and respect, whatever their institution or State.

Mission · Article 7

To bring radiation oncology residents and young radiation oncologists together, reduce the disparities between them, raise the standard of their education, and stand up for their welfare, pay and dignity.

What ROAR will do

Unity

Members in every State and every training institution, on one platform.

Reduce disparities

Narrow the gaps between institutions in training, exposure, stipends, hours and treatment.

Equal exposure

Fair access to IMRT/VMAT, IGRT, SBRT/SRS, brachytherapy, contouring and planning for every resident.

Standard of education

Raise the standard of RO education across India, and help members whose training falls short.

Welfare & dignity

Protect hours, stipends, salaries, leave, safety and mental health, and oppose mistreatment.

Advocacy & evidence

Annual national surveys, published results, and representation before NMC, NBEMS, States and AERB.

Working together · Article 42

Part of a wider radiation oncology community.

ROAR is independent, and it seeks to collaborate with national and international societies through memoranda of understanding, joint sessions and shared resources.

National societies

Joint academic sessions and shared resources with India's radiation oncology bodies, including AROI and its Young Radiation Oncology Forum, ICRO and the Association of Brachytherapy.

International societies

Links with ESTRO, ASTRO and FARO, so that Indian residents can take part in international education, fellowships and research.

Institutions

Partnerships with training departments for observerships, contouring sessions and multi-centre research.

Governance · Part IV

How ROAR is governed.

Every elected voting seat and every committee chair is held by a member within the voting window. Elected members always outnumber the founders on the Executive Council: 16 elected seats to 9.

General Body

Sovereign

All members within the voting window, with the Founders' Council. It elects the leadership, adopts the accounts and amends the constitution.

Executive Council

Governs and runs the work

The President, General Secretary, six Additional Secretaries, six Regional and two Resident Representatives, and the nine founders. At least three voting seats are always held by current residents.

Founders' Council

Guards ROAR's aims

The nine founders, for life. They can review any decision and pause one that goes against ROAR's aims for up to 30 days, but they don't run day-to-day work or spend funds. Disagreements are settled by vote, never by one person.

Advisory Mentor Board

Guidance and experience

Up to seven senior radiation oncologists who guide members on academic programmes, research and institutional links. Mentors serve in an advisory role.

The first two years

Until the first national election, decisions rest with the nine founders and the first 20 Founding Members together. The first Executive Cabinet stays in place for that period. From the end of year 2, the President is elected every year.

Principles

Independent, transparent, accountable.

Youth control is protected

The voting window, the founders' role, and the rule that mentors don't vote can only be changed by a three-quarters e-ballot of all voting members, with at least 40% turnout.

Independent of industry

Industry support is accepted only as unrestricted educational grants. Sponsors can't influence elections, position statements or advocacy, and no single sponsor may fund too large a share of anything.

Open books

Accounts are audited every year by a Chartered Accountant and published to members. Every sponsorship is listed in the annual report.

Money for welfare

A fixed share of ROAR's yearly income is set aside for welfare work: surveys, the grievance cell, legal advice and a hardship fund.

Conflicts declared

Office-bearers and faculty declare relevant financial relationships every year and step away from decisions where they have an interest.

No term-for-life

No one holds the same post for more than two consecutive terms, or sits on the Executive Council for more than six consecutive years.

Legal status

Registration is under way.

ROAR was founded on 5 November 2026. The founders are registering it as a Society under the Societies Registration Act, 1860, in New Delhi. The constitution is a working draft until then.
